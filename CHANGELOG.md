# Changelog

All published packages share one version: `herceg`, `@herceg/react`,
`@herceg/vue`, `@herceg/svelte`, and the Python package `herceg`. Before 1.0,
a minor version may change APIs; each such change is listed here with what
to do about it.

## Unreleased

The first public release. What it contains:

**Engine (`herceg`)**
- **Data and specs:**
  - columnar tables of typed arrays, with appends, rolling windows, replacements, and row views;
  - categories, times, derived tables, and portable expressions;
  - a serializable chart spec (`herceg/spec`) that every surface shares.
- **Marks:**
  - line, area, bar, point, heatmap, histogram, box plot, letter-value (boxen) plot, violin, error bar, candlestick, rug, and dendrogram;
  - rule, region, and text annotations;
  - pie, treemap, sunburst, gauge, funnel, radar, Sankey, network, maps (shapes and points), and 3D points, lines, and surfaces;
  - custom marks;
  - matplotlib dash strings (`"-"`, `"--"`, `":"`, `"-."`) on lines, rules, and custom shape styles, scaled by stroke width.
- **Composition:** marks compose in one chart, in facets, and in subplots, with shared or independent scales, right axes, and range navigators; 3D layers share CPU-rendered subplots with independent cameras and controls (3D facets are refused).
- **Layouts of panels (`herceg/layout`):** subplots go where a layout puts them: `columns(widths, children)`, `rows(heights, children)`, `grid({ rows, columns, gap, id }, cells)` with `cell({ row, column, rowSpan, columnSpan }, content)`, and `mosaic(rows of ids)`, nested as deep as needed, given to `panels(list, layout)` or `panels(layout)` with each panel's settings where it goes (`panel({ id?, title, x, y, ... })`; ids left out are `"panel-1"`, `"panel-2"`, ... by declaration order). Tracks are weights, `{ weight, min }`, or `{ px }`, and size the panels' plots, whose edges line up across tracks; a layout that does not fit is refused with the room it needs. Scopes take `{ layout, by }` and `{ panels }` beside `"chart"`, `"panel"`, `"row"`, and `"column"`, and axes take `repeat: "outer" | "all"`. In specs, the chart's `layout`.
- **Panels have scales of their own by default:** in subplots, x and y are each panel's unless a scope shares them (x is the chart's with a range navigator); facets share them.
- **Insets:** `inset(ref, { place, width, height, indicator })` draws a panel over another's plot, with scales of its own (the chart's `"chart"` scope leaves insets out), its axes in its box, and optionally an indicator of its ranges on the parent joined to it; the pointer, wheel, and brush act in the topmost panel, crosshairs and data stop at insets, and every renderer, export, and server snapshot draws them.
- **Panels' legends:** `legend({ panel })` lists what one panel draws and hides and shows it there only; the chart's legend lists what panels' legends leave out (`legend: true` keeps everything) and shows an entry hidden in some panels as mixed. `setVisibility(keys, visible, { panel })`, `getHiddenKeys({ panel })`, and the `visibility` event's `panels` carry visibility per panel.
- **Scales:** `x`, `y`, and `yRight` are the scales "x", "y", and "yRight" with their axes. Their `scope` (`"chart"`, `"panel"`, `"row"`, or `"column"`) says which facet panels or subplots share one instance; it replaces the chart's `resolve` option (Python: `hg.AxisOptions(scope=...)` replaces `resolve_x`, `resolve_y`, and `resolve_y_right`). A scale's type comes from the marks of the panels sharing it, so one subplot can show categories along an axis while another shows numbers.
- **Scales and guides:** `scales` (`{ x, y, yRight }`, scale settings over the short forms') and `guides` (`axis(...)`, `legend(...)`, `colorbar(...)`) add axes, legends, and colorbars beyond the automatic ones. Ticks are a count, `{ every }` (calendar steps such as `"6 hours"` or `"month"` in the chart's time zone, or numbers from an anchor), `{ values }`, or a function; formats are Intl options, `{ number }` d3-like patterns, `{ time }` strftime patterns, or a function; specs refuse functions at their path. Axes take tick layers (minor ticks, day boundaries in a second row) with tick direction and length, label rotation candidates, and collisions (`"hide"`, `"stagger"`, `"wrap"`, `"show"`), and go on the top (x) or right (y). Legends take `columns` or `rows`, `order`, a `title`, custom `entries`, `merge: "semantic"` (one entry per series value across marks and panels), and `swatches: "mark"`; marks take `legend: false`. Colorbars draw a continuous color scale along its domain, vertical or horizontal, with ticks, a title, and a format, or a contour's bands as steps, and are described with their ranges. The guide compiler loads only with charts that use these. Python: `hg.Scale`, `hg.ScaleRef`, `hg.Ticks`, `hg.Format`, `hg.Tick`, `hg.TickLabels`, `hg.TickLayer`, `hg.axis`, `hg.legend`, `hg.colorbar`, `hg.LegendEntry`, `hg.Swatch`, and `legend=False` on marks; `hg.AxisOptions` and `hg.LegendOptions` are the short forms' settings.
- **Styles and themes:** a theme is tokens (`background`, `text`, `muted`, `grid`, `axis`, `palette`, `sequential`, `diverging`, `rise`, `fall`) and role styles (`text`, `title`, `panelTitle`, `axisTitle`, `tickLabel`, `legendLabel`, `legendTitle`, `plot`, `grid`, `axisLine`, `tick`, and the marks' `line`, `bar`, and `tooltip` defaults) over the built-in theme it `extends`: `theme: { extends, tokens, style }`, which replaces the theme's top-level keys (`font`, `fontSize`, `titleSize`, `gridDash`, `lineWidth`, `barRadius`, `tooltipBackground`, `tooltipBorder` become `style` roles). Axes, the legend, the title, and subplots take a `style` of their roles, cascading over the theme's. Text styles set font, size, weight, slant, color, opacity, line height, alignment, halo, a background box, and padding (the box goes where its text would go without one, and the text moves in by the padding); line styles set stroke, width, dash, and opacity; the plot style a background and border. Python: `hg.Theme`, `hg.Tokens`, `hg.Style`, `hg.TextStyle`, `hg.StrokeStyle`, `hg.PlotStyle`, `hg.LineStyle`, `hg.BarStyle`, `hg.TooltipStyle`, `hg.TextBox`.
- **Layout:** `padding` (pixels, or per side; `0` lets a chart fill its size; left out, spacing is automatic); titles as a string or `{ text, place, style }`, at the top or bottom, aligned by their style, broken into lines at `"\n"`; subplot titles as a string or `{ text, style }`; legends as `true`, `false`, or `{ place, style }`, placed on any side (`align`, `offset`) or inside a plot's corner or edge (`inset`, `scope`, `panel`). Python: `hg.Title`, `hg.LegendOptions`, `hg.Place`, `padding=`.
- **Marks:** `clip` (`"plot"`, `"chart"`, or `false`) on marks on the chart's scales; `halo` (`"auto"`, `false`, or `{ color, width?, opacity? }`) on `text`, `region`, `bar`, `funnel`, `radar`, `sankey`, `graph`, and `rangeNavigator`, replacing its boolean form. Python: `clip=`, and `halo=` taking `"auto"`, `False`, or `hg.Halo`.
- **Fonts (`herceg/fonts`):** `prepareFonts(sources)` gives a chart and every export the same TrueType faces (`fonts` on chart options, `renderToSVG`, `specToSVG`, `renderToPDF`, and `specToPDF`): live charts measure with them and register them with the page, SVG and PNG exports measure with them, PDF embeds subsets, and text in them is drawn unkerned, as measured. `fallback` and `missingGlyph` are PDF export settings. It replaces `pdfFonts` from `herceg/pdf` (`FontSource`, `FontOptions`, and `Fonts` replace `PdfFontSource`, `PdfFontOptions`, and `PdfFonts`).
- **Positions:** the portable `Coordinate` (a data value on a named scale, a field, a fraction of the plot or chart, or pixels from its edge, with a pixel offset) and `Position` (one per axis) types; annotations' number-or-field positions are typed `AnnotationValue`.
- **Encodings:** `color` is a mark's primary paint (a line's stroke, a bar's, area's, or point's fill): a category field through the chart's `"color"` scale, or a CSS color; `stroke` and `fill` win over it. `encode` takes channels by field and named scale, constant, or condition (`{ condition: { when, then, otherwise } }` over portable expressions) for color, fill, stroke, dash, strokeWidth, and opacity on lines, areas, and bars. Scales of categories (`scales: { color: { type: "ordinal", domain, map, unknown } }`) give a value one output in every mark and panel, before facets and hidden series, in the domain's or the dictionary's order, and keep it while data streams. Points' category colors go through the color scale, in the field's dictionary order. `style.by` maps series (or bars' rows) to looks by a field's value or by series name; lines take `markers` and `style.marker`, and their looks show in legends with `swatches: "mark"`; `opacity` on lines, areas, and bars, and `strokeOpacity` on those and rules. Python: `hg.Channel`, `hg.Encodings`, `hg.when`, `hg.OrdinalScale(...)`, `hg.MarkStyle`, `hg.StyleMap`, `hg.SeriesStyle`, `hg.MarkerStyle`.
- **Color implies series:** a category `color` field splits lines, areas (range areas too), bars, and points (along axes, on maps, and in 3D) into series, as `series` does, unless the mark gives `series` (`series: false` for none, which colors bars and points along axes row by row; map and 3D points refuse it) or its own stroke or fill; histograms, box plots, boxen plots, violins, error bars, and rugs take `color` too, a category field naming their series or a color. A line or area whose color field changes within a series is refused, also as rows stream in (checking only the new rows). Marks without `color` refuse it, naming what to give instead. Python: the same in `hg.*` marks (`series=False`), and `herceg.express` helpers take `color=` as their series column, before statistics.
- **Color identity:** a value has one color and one legend entry in every mark and panel, layout marks' items included (pie slices, top-level tree branches, radar series, map regions and points, network node groups, 3D points and lines), following tables made from one table back to it (derived tables, row views, and spec tables naming their `origin`); unrelated tables keep their own unless a named scale joins them (`encode.color: { field, scale }`, or `{ series: true, scale }` for series' names); value fields stand for their labels. Colors stay while data is appended, dropped, replaced, and hidden.
- **Legends by identity:** a legend lists one entry per value, which toggles every series standing for it, with each mark's look under `swatches: "mark"`. This replaces the legend's `merge: "semantic"` and marks' `legendGroup` (Python `merge="semantic"` and `legend_group`), which are removed: drop them; marks of one field share entries without them.
- **Errors you can see:** `ChartError` (`code`, `phase`, `path`, `mark`, `panel`; `errorInfo` as JSON) for what a chart refuses, in one shape for every surface. A chart that cannot draw shows a box of its size saying "Chart could not be rendered" and why, or over a last frame still right, "Chart update failed; showing previous data.", and reports it once to `onError`, `chart.on("error")`, and `chart.ready`; `errorMode: "throw"` throws instead. Exports (`toSVG`, `toPNG`) throw what keeps a frame from drawing.
- **Line breaks and conditional bands:** `continuity: { defined, maxGap, breakBy }` on lines and areas decides which observations connect before they are reduced, so reduced lines never bridge a break; `where` leaves rows out as gaps, ending runs where the condition changes (interpolated on linear curves, exact on step curves), so conditional bands meet their lines, in time axis breaks leave and on log scales too. Value domains, text summaries, and tooltips leave those rows out; positions' domains keep every row. Python: `hg.Continuity`, `hg.Duration` (or `timedelta`), `break_by=hg.col(...)` for a field, `where=`.
- **Bars:** series stand side by side at each position, as before, unless `dodge: false` overlaps them or `stack` piles them; `series` partitions and `dodge` places on every mark, with a default of its own for bars (points, lines, areas, and error bars dodge only when asked). A category `color` field splits bars into series (see Color implies series); with `series: false`, each bar takes its row's color and keeps its whole band. Bars take `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `strokeOpacity`, `dash`, and `opacity`; colored by a field other than their series', they list its values in a key. Value labels are `true` or a label object `{ field, format, style, minLength, place }`, replacing Intl options (now `format`), inside the bars with `place: { outside: false, attachment }`; labels inside bars are white wherever white reaches 3:1 contrast, else black. Python: `hg.BarLabels`.
- **Annotations:** places along an axis are values, fields, or coordinates (`{ data }`, `{ field }`, `{ fraction }`, `{ px }`, each with an `offset`); text takes `at: { x, y }`; rules take a `span` and `strokeOpacity`. `label` is text or a label object `{ text | field, format, style, place }`, replacing `textField`, and for text `anchor`, `fill`, `dx`, and `dy` (use `label: { field }`, `style: { align, color }`, and coordinate offsets). A rule's label can go outside the plot beside the y axis, in room the chart keeps for it (`place: { attachment: "start", outside: true }`). Annotations no longer widen the axes' domains unless they say `contributeDomain: true`, so they show where the data is: thresholds meant to stay in view say so, or the chart sets a domain. Python: `hg.Label`, `hg.LabelPlace`, `hg.Position`, `hg.Fraction`, `hg.Pixels`, `hg.DataPosition`, `contribute_domain=`; `text_field`, `anchor`, `fill`, `dx`, and `dy` are gone.
- **Stacks:** an area's `stack` takes `{ offset, order, gaps }`, replacing `stackOrder` and `stackGaps` (Python: `hg.Stack`, replacing `stack_order` and `stack_gaps`).
- **Symbols:** points and markers take `"x"`, a cross turned 45 degrees; `"cross"` stays a plus.
- **Rendering:** SVG or Canvas chosen per chart, WebGL for large point clouds and standalone 3D charts, and level-of-detail reduction for large lines and areas.
- **Interaction:**
  - tooltips with configurable fields;
  - keyboard reading;
  - zoom and pan;
  - brushes and lassos;
  - row selections shared between charts, which summaries (histograms, box plots, violins, heatmaps, pies, funnels, trees, Sankey diagrams) show as each item's selected part, in exports too;
  - synced cursors and views;
  - drilldown, map and network navigation, and node dragging;
  - per-panel 3D cameras through `getCamera({ panel })`, `setCamera(camera, { panel })`, and scoped camera events.
- **Gestures and bindings:** `interaction.bindings` binds gestures (the wheel by axis and keys held, or only while the chart has the focus; drags by mouse button and kind of pointer; pinches; keys; double clicks) to actions (`pan`, `zoom`, `brush`, `reset`, `cursor`, `orbit`, `moveItem`, `pageScroll`), tried in order before the presets. `pan`, `zoom`, `brush`, `rotate`, and `drag` are presets of bindings, and maps, networks, trees, and 3D charts match the same bindings as axis charts. Duplicate gestures, the keyboard cursor's keys without Ctrl, Alt, or Meta, and brushes that zoom while selecting rows are refused. Python: `bindings=[hg.Binding(gesture, action)]` with `hg.WheelGesture`, `hg.DragGesture`, `hg.PinchGesture`, `hg.KeyGesture`, `hg.DoubleClickGesture`, and `hg.PanAction`, `hg.ZoomAction`, `hg.BrushAction`, `hg.ResetAction`, `hg.CursorAction`, `hg.OrbitAction`, `hg.MoveItemAction`, `hg.PageScrollAction`.
- **Behavior changes in interaction:**
  - the vertical wheel scrolls the page over every chart; it zooms only with Ctrl or ⌘ held (as trackpad pinches send it), in axis charts, maps, networks, and 3D charts alike, and a touch pinch zooms charts that zoom;
  - the horizontal wheel, and Shift with the wheel, pan x in charts that pan or zoom along x;
  - `interaction.pan` (`true`, `"x"`, `"y"`, `"xy"`) pans by dragging without zooming; `zoom` still pans by dragging, unless `pan: false`; layout-only charts (pies, treemaps, gauges, ...) refuse `pan`;
  - `+` (or `=`) and `-` zoom by 1.25 about the plot's middle and `0` resets in axis charts that zoom, as in maps and networks;
  - double-click resets in charts that pan, as in charts that zoom;
  - Escape during a drag ends it: a brush drawn so far is dropped, and a pan goes back to where it started;
  - touch: charts that brush or pan along one axis leave pinches to the page (`touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` rather than `pan-y`), and charts that zoom take them;
  - the code that answers input (tooltips, the pointer, the wheel, keys, the keyboard cursor, crosshairs) loads right after the chart's first frame, or at its first input; input that arrives earlier is kept and handled once it loads, and `chart.loading` is pending until then, so tests that check what input does right after creating a chart await it;
  - crosshair labels on the axes (`tooltip.axisLabels`) are HTML boxes placed by CSS, and moving the cursor measures no text;
  - the cursor shows across a chart's panels that share an x scale (facets, subplots): where the pointer or keyboard is in one, the others show a crosshair and their points at the same x (`sync: { panels: false }` turns it off);
  - the keyboard's cursor is shared with synced charts, as the pointer's is;
  - synced selections carry categories by name rather than by index, so charts whose categories differ or come in another order share them; a selection of categories arrives as the range from the first to the last of them in the receiving chart's order.
- **View limits:** `interaction.limits` keeps each scale's view inside an `extent` (`{ x: { extent: [from, null] } }`), however it is asked for (the pointer, keys, `setViewport`, brushes, synced charts; a view fitted to the data shows the data), and with `edge` reports an `edge` event (`scale`, `side`, `domain`, `bound`, `panels`, `cause`) when the view comes near an end (the extent's, or on a shared x scale, the data's), again only after it moved away by `hysteresis` or the end moved, so applications load history as the reader pans back while the view holds. Events say what moved the view (`ViewCause`). Adapters: `onEdge`, `@edge`, `onedge`. Python: `limits={"x": hg.ViewLimits(extent=..., edge=True)}`, `hg.Edge`, and `widget.on_edge` with `hg.ViewEdge`.
- **Sync channels:** `sync` takes `{ cursor, viewport, selection }`, each a group's name or `{ group, scale }`, so charts share the cursor alone, or follow another chart's x along their y; a group's name alone shares all three along x, as before. Values travel as data, and charts never share again what they receive. The cursor's channel takes `snap` (`"nearest"`, the default; `"previous"`, each series' last point at or before the shared value, for readings at other frequencies; `"next"`) and `tooltip: true` (the receiving chart's own tooltip at the cursor, one to a chart). `sync.panels` shares the cursor across the chart's own panels (`true`, `false`, or `{ snap, tooltip }`; on by default for panels sharing x). `chart.showCursor({ x }, { snap, tooltip })` and `chart.hideCursor()` show and hide the cursor as a synced chart's (shared unless `{ broadcast: false }`). Python: `sync=hg.Sync(cursor=..., viewport=..., selection=..., panels=...)` with `hg.SyncChannel(group, scale=, snap=, tooltip=)`, and `widget.show_cursor(x=..., snap=..., tooltip=...)` and `widget.hide_cursor()`.
- **Server rendering:** `renderToSnapshot(options, { width, height, idPrefix, fonts, locale, timeZone })` renders a chart without a DOM, as the browser runtime builds it, with deterministic ids and what hydrating needs, and `hydrateChart(host, options, snapshot)` takes that markup over without drawing it again (drawing only what differs, keeping the markup while code it waits for loads, and refusing markup the snapshot does not describe). `specToSnapshot` does so for specs. React, Vue, and Svelte `<Chart>` take `initial={hydration(snapshot)}` and hydrate without a mismatch or flash; `hydration` brings the adoption code, which other pages do not carry. Python: `chart.to_snapshot(id_prefix=...)`.
- **Accessibility:** generated descriptions, keyboard navigation, and accessible legends; tested with axe-core in Chrome, Firefox, and WebKit.
- **Export:** static SVG, PNG, and tagged multi-page PDF, without a DOM where possible; fluid SVG with `sizing: "fluid"`, `preserveAspectRatio`, and `strokeScaling: "fixed"` (`renderToSVG`, `specToSVG`, `chart.toSVG(sizing)`; Python `to_svg(sizing=..., preserve_aspect_ratio=..., stroke_scaling=...)`).
- **Analytical grid (`herceg/grid`):** sorting, filtering, grouping, pivots, remote data, and row selection.

**Framework adapters:** `@herceg/react`, `@herceg/vue`, and `@herceg/svelte`. Charts are written as components, while data updates bypass the framework. Errors show in the chart's place, reach `onError` (`@error`, `onerror`), and with `errorMode="throw"` are thrown in render for error boundaries. Each adapter's `/layout` has `Columns`, `Rows`, `Grid`, `Cell`, `Panel`, and `Inset` components, whose marks take the panel they are in, and `/guides` has `Legend`, a panel's own inside a `Panel`.

**Python (`herceg`)**
- `hg.*` charts from pandas, Polars, PyArrow, and NumPy data, sent as binary columns;
- output to notebooks, HTML, SVG, PNG, and PDF;
- layouts of subplots built cell by cell: `chart.columns([2, 1])`, `rows`, `grid(rows=, columns=)` with `cell(row=, column=, row_span=, column_span=)`, and `mosaic([...])` give cells whose `panel(id=None, weight=, title=, ...)` makes a panel taking mark methods (`latency.line(...)`), insets (`panel.inset(...)`), and a legend of its own (`panel.legend(...)`); `layout=hg.Grid(columns=2)` places registered panels; tracks take `hg.Weight(n, min=)` and `hg.Pixels(n)`;
- live notebook widgets and a Streamlit component, with camera state per 3D panel, and each panel's own view and selection where panels have x scales of their own (`widget.panel_viewports`, `on_viewport(..., panel=)`, `hg.Selection(panels=...)`; facet panels with x scales of their own are refused in widgets);
- `herceg.express`: Seaborn-style statistical charts with recompute on new data; the functions drawing one plot take `panel=`, a panel of another chart, which they return (`hx.statistics(chart, panel=...)`);
- helpful unknown-option errors for matplotlib names, restricted to the callable's supported options;
- `hg.ChartError`, a `ValueError` with the engine's code and path, raised as marks are added (unknown columns, listing those there are; column kinds; inferred series with several value columns), as charts are output (scale references), and from the engine; HTML, notebooks, widgets, and Streamlit show a chart that cannot draw in its place.
